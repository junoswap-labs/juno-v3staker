Executive summary

JunoswapV3Staker is a fork of Uniswap's v3-staker (v1.0.2) that replaces the reward model. Upstream measures a position against the liquidity-seconds of the *whole pool*, so an incentive on a pool where only a slice of the TVL is staked pays out only that slice of its budget. The fork replaces this with a fixed drip of totalReward / duration shared among the *staked* positions via a rewardPerLiquidityX128 accumulator, then scales each payout down by the fraction of the stake's life the position spent in range. Everything else — access control, deposit custody, the incentive key, the transfer helpers — is upstream, unchanged.

The access-control and custody surface is clean. There is no owner, no admin, no upgrade path, no pause and no sweep function: createIncentive is permissionless and self-funding, every state-changing function checks deposits[tokenId].owner or msg.sender, and checks-effects-interactions is respected on both token-moving paths. Liquidity-provider principal is never at risk: the LP NFT comes back on a path that touches only the position manager and the pool, and we could not construct a state in which that path reverts. Arithmetic conserves — payouts round down, the accumulator is bounded by the type(uint128).max reward cap, and no denominator can reach zero.

The findings are all in the new reward model, and all three High-severity ones share one root: in-range status is applied as a post-hoc multiplier on the payout instead of being part of the accumulator's denominator. That single choice produces (H-01) a payout ratio whose numerator keeps growing after endTime while its denominator stops there, letting a position that was never in range during the incentive collect its whole share by waiting; (H-02) out-of-range liquidity that dilutes every honest staker while being paid nothing, so a position costing 0.0000019 tokens cuts a real LP's share of the budget to 8.7e-14 of it; and (H-03) the permissionless out-of-range eviction added to work around H-02, which keys on a spot tick that a single swap can set and so becomes a tool for clearing every competing staker out of the incentive.

The value these findings move is always the reward budget, never LP principal, and in each case it lands with key.refundee or with an attacker rather than with the intended participants — which is precisely what the fork exists to fix.

No Critical findings. 3 High, 2 Medium, 1 Low, 1 Informational (bundled). Nine candidates were raised during the read and seven survived the refutation pass.

---

Post-audit remediation. Five of the seven findings were fixed in the same session and are marked Fixed below: H-01, H-02, H-03, M-02 and L-01 — every issue in roadmap tiers 0, 1 and 2. The contract compiles clean on solc 0.7.6 and the accounting model passes, extended from 8 cases to 12 to cover the fixed behaviour.

One correction worth recording, because it changed the fix: the first attempt at H-01 was the one-line clamp this report originally recommended, and the project's own model test 7 rejected it. The clamp is a sound lower bound but loosens by a second per second of delay, so it zeroed an honest staker who merely unstook late — trading a leak against the refundee for a leak against LPs. The shipped fix pairs the clamp with finalizeStake, a permissionless, batchable call that freezes a stake's in-range measurement at endTime and makes the discount vanish. The recommendation text in H-01 has been updated to match what was actually built.

M-01 was left open on purpose — a minimum range width is a product decision, not a bug. I-01 remains open; none of it is a defect.

The fixes are reviewed only against this report's own model and the compiler. They have not been tested against a real Uniswap V3 pool, and the fork tests, invariant tests and Slither run in roadmap tier 3 are still outstanding — finalizeStake and the two new _stakeToken guards are new code paths that no on-chain test exercises.

H-01 · In-range seconds accrued after endTime are credited against a window that stops at endTime

Fixed

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:230-239 (and the same expression in getRewardInfo at :292-301); libraries/RewardMath.sol:38-53

Description

unstakeToken builds the payout as accumulatorShare × secondsInside / stakedSeconds. The two sides of that ratio measure different windows.

The denominator is capped at the incentive's end: _accrue returns currentTime = min(block.timestamp, endTime), and stakedSeconds is derived from it. The numerator is not capped at all — secondsInside is read from pool.snapshotCumulativesInside at the moment of the call, and that counter keeps growing for as long as the position stays staked and the pool price sits inside its range, including every second after endTime.

So a stake accumulates in-range credit indefinitely after the incentive is over, against a denominator that stopped growing when the incentive ended. RewardMath.computeRewardAmount clamps the ratio at 1.0, which is what turns a slow leak into a full one: a position that was in range for *none* of the incentive can reach a ratio of exactly 1.0 and collect its entire accumulator share, simply by remaining staked until the pool price has spent stakedSeconds more seconds inside its range.

Nothing has to be manipulated for this to happen, but it can be. Once the incentive is over there is no cost to swapping the pool into one's own range and waiting — and the position holder chooses when to call unstakeToken, so they can wait for exactly the ratio they want.

Upstream does not have this. Uniswap's RewardMath grows its denominator by Math.max(endTime, currentTime) - startTime, so a late unstaker's numerator and denominator inflate together and the reward converges rather than climbing. The fork dropped that term when it rewrote the model.

Verified numerically against the project's own test/reward-model.test.js accounting class. An incentive of 1,000,000 over 1,000 seconds with one staker that is never in range during the incentive pays 0 if closed out at endTime, and the full 1,000,000 if closed out 1,000 seconds later with the price inside its range for that period. The project's own case 7 covers the in-range-throughout variant of this scenario and passes; the out-of-range variant is untested.

Vulnerable code
(, , uint32 secondsInside) = key.pool.snapshotCumulativesInside(deposit.tickLower, deposit.tickUpper); uint256 reward =    RewardMath.computeRewardAmount(        incentive.rewardPerLiquidityX128 - stake.rewardPerLiquidityInitialX128,        stake.liquidity,        currentTime > stake.stakeTime ? currentTime - stake.stakeTime : 0,        // numerator keeps growing after endTime; the denominator above does not        uint256(uint32(secondsInside - stake.secondsInsideInitial)),        incentive.totalRewardUnclaimed    );
Impact

A staked position collects reward for uptime it never had, taking it from totalRewardUnclaimed — that is, out of the refund endIncentive owes key.refundee. For a launchpad, the refundee is the launchpad itself, so this is real money, not an accounting curiosity.

The over-credit is min(1, p + s/D) − p, where p is the position's true in-range fraction during the incentive, D the incentive duration and s the in-range seconds it accrues after endTime. For a 30-day incentive: one day of post-end in-range time over-credits by 3% of the position's share, one week by 23%, and a full month takes any position to 100% of its share regardless of what it actually did.

Other stakers are not directly diluted — the accumulator conserves, so each staker's share is fixed and the excess is taken only from the forfeited pool. The loss falls entirely on the refundee.

The mitigating factor is that unstakeToken becomes permissionless at endTime, so anyone — including the refundee — can close a stake out and freeze its ratio. That makes this a race rather than a guarantee, which is why it is High and not Critical. But it requires the refundee to be watching and to transact promptly for every outstanding stake, and endIncentive cannot even be called until all of them are closed.

Recommendation

Bound the credited in-range seconds by what could not possibly have accrued after the incentive ended. In-range seconds accrue at most one per wall-clock second, so at most block.timestamp − endTime of the observed growth is post-end; subtracting that yields a sound lower bound on the true pre-endTime in-range time, with no new storage and no new external call.

This errs against the late unstaker, which is the safe direction and reinforces the incentive to close stakes out promptly that the permissionless post-end unstake already creates. Apply the identical change to getRewardInfo so the view keeps matching the state-changing path.

A fuller fix is to stop measuring in-range status after the fact at all and move it into the accumulator's denominator — see H-02, whose remediation subsumes this one.

Applied. Both halves shipped together, because the clamp alone is not enough. _measureUptime discounts the reading by block.timestamp - endTime, which is a sound lower bound on the in-range time genuinely earned inside the reward window — but it loosens by one second per second of delay, so on its own it zeroes an honest staker who simply unstakes late. The project's own model test 7 (unstaking long after endTime earns nothing extra) catches exactly that regression, and it is a real one: under-crediting an honest LP is not an acceptable price for closing the leak.

finalizeStake resolves it. Anyone may freeze a stake's in-range measurement from endTime, once, in a call cheap enough to batch every outstanding stake through multicall. Called in the block endTime passes, the discount is zero and the reading is exact, so honest stakes are paid in full however late they are unstaked. The two new Stake fields pack into the existing slot (232 of 256 bits), so this costs no extra storage.

Freezing gives an attacker no lever in either direction: a position that was out of range during the incentive measures zero whenever it finalizes, and finalizing later only tightens the discount. Model case 12 asserts this across three freeze times.

Recommended fix

H-02 · Out-of-range staked liquidity dilutes every honest staker, and the forfeited share goes to the refundee instead of being redistributed

Fixed

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:341-360 (_stakeToken), :307-319 (_accrue); libraries/RewardMath.sol:19-27

Description

_stakeToken adds every position's liquidity to incentive.stakedLiquidity with no regard for whether the pool price is inside its range, and _accrue divides the drip by that total. The in-range correction is then applied at the *other* end, as a per-position multiplier on the payout.

The consequence is that an out-of-range position takes a full share out of the denominator while being paid nothing for it — and its share is not handed to the stakers who *were* in range. It stays in totalRewardUnclaimed and is paid to key.refundee by endIncentive.

Upstream does not behave this way. Uniswap's secondsPerLiquidityInsideX128 is normalised by the pool's *in-range* liquidity, so a position sitting outside the current price contributes nothing to anyone's denominator. The fork's rewrite moved the range test from the denominator to the payout and lost that property.

This is a real cost in ordinary operation — on any pool whose price moves, staked positions drift out of range and silently suppress everyone else's yield. It is also cheap to weaponise. Uniswap caps a single position's liquidity at maxLiquidityPerTick = type(uint128).max / numTicks, which is 1.1505e34 at the 0.3% fee tier, roughly thirteen orders of magnitude above a well-capitalised real position. A position that far out of range costs almost nothing to mint: amount0 = L × (1/√a − 1/√b) for a 60-tick-wide range at the top of the tick range comes to 1.879e12 wei — 0.0000019 tokens at 18 decimals.

Verified numerically with the project's own accounting model: a staker with L = 1e21, in range for the entire incentive, receives 0 of a 1,000,000 budget once one such position is staked alongside it. The entire budget is refunded to key.refundee.

The existing defence is the permissionless out-of-range unstake in unstakeToken, which lets anyone evict the dust position. It is a partial one: re-staking costs the attacker a single transaction, and staking k dust positions in one multicall forces k separate unstakeToken calls to clear. The eviction economics are asymmetric in the attacker's favour, and on a cheap-gas chain the war is affordable indefinitely. That defence is also itself a finding — see H-03.

Refuted during review: the dilution cannot be applied retroactively. _accrue settles the accumulator using the pre-existing stakedLiquidity before the new stake is added, so a position staked and unstaked in the same block dilutes nothing. The attacker must hold the position continuously across the interval it wants to suppress.

Vulnerable code
require(pool == key.pool, 'JunoswapV3Staker::stakeToken: token pool is not the incentive pool');require(liquidity > 0, 'JunoswapV3Staker::stakeToken: cannot stake token with 0 liquidity');// nothing checks that the position is in range, or that its liquidity is of a sane size:// a 0.0000019-token position at maxLiquidityPerTick (1.15e34) crushes every honest staker _accrue(incentive, key); deposits[tokenId].numberOfStakes++;incentive.numberOfStakes++;incentive.stakedLiquidity += liquidity;
Impact

In ordinary operation the fraction of the budget that reaches stakers equals the liquidity-weighted in-range uptime of the staked set — a single permanently-out-of-range peer of equal size halves an honest LP's yield, and the difference is returned to the refundee rather than redistributed. This defeats the contract's stated purpose, which is that a launchpad's fixed reward reaches its participants.

Under attack, an incentive can be denied its distribution almost entirely for the cost of gas plus dust: one maxLiquidityPerTick position drops an L = 1e21 staker to 8.7e-14 of the budget. The attacker gains nothing directly — this is griefing — but the damage (an entire reward budget) is orders of magnitude above the cost, so it does not qualify for the Medium band where attacker cost is comparable to damage. LP principal is never at risk.

Recommendation

Two changes close the cheap attack with a small diff, and a third is the architectural fix.

1. Require a position to be in range to enter. A one-line require in _stakeToken removes the entry point for a position that is out of range from the moment it is staked, which is the whole of the dust attack.

2. Bound the credited liquidity against the pool's own active liquidity. pool.liquidity() is the in-range liquidity right now — a real, market-sized number. Requiring a stake not to exceed it makes a maxLiquidityPerTick position unstakeable unless the pool genuinely holds that much, which no pool does. Together with (1) this is the practical fix, and it is a smaller diff than reworking the accumulator.

3. Architecturally, stop correcting for range after the fact and keep out-of-range liquidity out of the denominator, so the forfeited share is redistributed among the stakers who were in range rather than returned to the refundee. That requires the denominator to track tick crossings, which is what the pool's secondsPerLiquidityInsideX128 already provides — reintroducing it for the numerator while keeping the fork's fixed drip is the shape worth designing toward. Whether the residue should go to in-range stakers or to the refundee is a product decision; the current code makes it implicitly, and it should be made explicitly.

Neither (1) nor (2) handles a position that drifts out of range *after* it is staked, which is why (3) matters and why H-03 exists.

Recommended fix

H-03 · Permissionless out-of-range unstake is keyed on a spot tick, letting one swap evict every competing staker

Fixed

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:212-219 (unstakeToken), :321-328 (_isOutOfRange)

Description

The fork relaxes upstream's owner-only rule: any caller may close another owner's stake whenever _isOutOfRange is true. _isOutOfRange reads slot0.tick — the instantaneous pool price — with no TWAP, no observation window and no requirement that the position has been out of range for any length of time. A single swap in the same transaction sets that value.

The attack is one transaction from an attacker-owned contract: swap the pool past the ranges of every competing staker, call unstakeToken for each of them (permissionless now, because the tick says they are out of range), then swap back. Concentrated positions have narrow ranges by definition, so the displacement needed is small and most of it is recovered on the return swap; the net cost is two fee payments plus unrecovered slippage.

The victims' accrued reward up to that moment is credited, so nothing already earned is lost. What they lose is the future: they stop accruing, and only the deposit owner can call stakeToken to get back in, so each victim must notice and pay to re-enter. Between the eviction and their return the attacker's own position is a larger fraction of stakedLiquidity and takes a correspondingly larger fraction of the drip.

One asymmetry makes it worse: _isOutOfRange can never be true for a full-range position, since tick < MIN_TICK is unreachable. An attacker staked at [MIN_TICK, MAX_TICK] is structurally immune to the mechanism it is using on everyone else, and stays in range through its own manipulation.

The same transaction is available as pure griefing with no position at all, at the cost of the swap round trip alone.

This path exists to work around H-02 — an out-of-range position that keeps diluting everyone else. That is a real problem, but a spot-tick test is not a safe way to detect it.

Impact

The reward drip is transferred from narrow-range LPs to the attacker for the duration of each eviction gap, and the attack is repeatable every block. Yield only — the deposit and the NFT are never at risk, and already-accrued reward is credited before the stake is closed.

Secondary effect: a victim who is evicted and does not notice earns nothing for the rest of the incentive while believing they are staked, and the reward they would have earned is forfeited to key.refundee under H-02 rather than redistributed.

Recommendation

Gate the eviction on a condition a single block cannot manufacture.

Preferred: require a minimum out-of-range dwell time rather than an instantaneous reading. The pool already provides the measurement — compare two snapshotCumulativesInside observations and require that secondsInside has not advanced over a window of, say, an hour. This tests the thing that actually matters (the position has genuinely stopped earning) instead of a proxy that is manipulable within one transaction.

Alternative: read a TWAP tick from pool.observe() over a fixed window instead of slot0.tick. Cheaper to implement, but it needs the pool's observation cardinality to be large enough for the window, which is not guaranteed and should be checked at incentive creation.

Also: consider removing the eviction path entirely once H-02 is fixed at source. Its only purpose is to limit the damage from out-of-range dilution; if out-of-range liquidity no longer dilutes, the path is a liability with no remaining benefit.

Applied, via the dwell-time option rather than the TWAP one. The eviction test is now stakedSeconds >= 1 hours && secondsInside * 10 <= stakedSeconds — the stake has existed long enough to judge, and has spent at most a tenth of that time in range. Both quantities are already computed for the payout, so this adds no external call, and unlike the TWAP option it carries no dependency on the pool's observation cardinality (where an insufficient window would make observe revert and disable eviction entirely).

It measures the harm directly rather than a proxy for it, and it cannot be manufactured in one transaction: a swap changes the current tick, but it cannot retroactively remove in-range seconds a position has already banked. A position that is genuinely earning is no longer evictable at all; one that has earned essentially nothing over an hour still is, which is what the path exists for.

M-01 · Reward weight is raw liquidity with no range-width floor, so $45 at the minimum width matches $100,000 at ±10%

Open

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:341-360 (_stakeToken); libraries/RewardMath.sol:19-27

Description

A position's share of the drip is its liquidity divided by stakedLiquidity, with no reference to how wide its range is. In Uniswap V3, liquidity per unit of deposited capital scales as the inverse of range width: for a position around price P spanning relative width W, the value deposited is roughly L × √P × W / 2, so L = 2V / (√P × W). Reward per dollar therefore scales as 1 / W, without limit down to the tick spacing.

The practical effect is that whoever posts the tightest range collects the budget. At the 0.01% fee tier, where tick spacing is 1 and the minimum width is 0.0001, a $45 position earns what a $100,000 position at ±10% earns. At the 0.30% tier the ratio is 36.9×, and even at 1.00% it is 11×.

The trade-off the LP accepts is that a minimum-width position falls out of range on almost any price movement, and under this contract that means both losing its own uptime multiplier and (under H-03) becoming evictable. But it can simply be re-minted around the new price, and while it is in range it dominates.

This is inherited from upstream, not introduced by the fork — Uniswap's own staker also weights by raw liquidity, and this is a known property of V3 liquidity mining generally. It is reported because the fork's stated design goal is that a launchpad's fixed reward reaches its participants, and this determines who those participants turn out to be.

Vulnerable code

Impact

The reward budget concentrates on whoever posts the tightest range with the most capital, rather than on the LPs providing usable depth. A launchpad believing it is rewarding its liquidity providers is in practice running an auction for the narrowest position. There is no theft and no loss of principal — the effect is on who receives the distribution, and the magnitude is set by the fee tier: 2,222× at 0.01%, 36.9× at 0.30%.

Filed at Medium: the value leak is real and bounded by the budget, and no exploit or privilege is needed, but it is a distribution-fairness property rather than a defect, and it is inherited rather than introduced.

Recommendation

This is a product decision and either answer is defensible, but it should be an explicit one rather than a consequence of the weighting formula.

Option A — minimum width. Require tickUpper - tickLower >= MIN_WIDTH_TICKS at stake time, with the floor set from the pool's fee tier and expected volatility, and stored as an immutable or per-incentive constructor parameter. Simplest to implement and easy for LPs to reason about.

Option B — width-weighted reward. Credit liquidity × (tickUpper − tickLower) instead of liquidity alone, which makes reward proportional to deposited value rather than to concentration. This changes the accumulator's units but not its structure, and it removes the incentive to narrow rather than merely capping it.

Option C — document it. If concentration is the intent, say so in the incentive's public documentation, because the current NatSpec implies the opposite.

Whichever is chosen, state the resulting distribution alongside the incentive so participants can price their position before committing capital.

Not applied — deliberately. This is a product decision about who the incentive is meant to reward, not a defect, and either answer is defensible. Picking a minRangeTicks on the team's behalf would bake in a judgement about the pool's expected volatility that this review has no basis for making.

M-02 · Per-incentive accounting assumes an exact-transfer ERC-20; a fee-on-transfer or rebasing reward token freezes claims across every incentive sharing it

Fixed

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:112-115 (createIncentive), :250-264 (claimReward)

Description

createIncentive credits incentive.totalReward and incentive.totalRewardUnclaimed with the *requested* reward and only then calls safeTransferFrom. It never checks how much actually arrived.

Incentive accounting is per-incentive, but the token balance backing it is global. rewards[rewardToken][owner] and the contract's balance of that token are shared by every incentive using the same reward token, so a shortfall in one is paid out of another's funds until the balance runs out — at which point claimReward reverts for whoever is left, with no way to recover.

Three token behaviours produce the shortfall:

  • Fee-on-transfer / deflationary. The contract receives reward × (1 − fee) while the books say reward. Because incentive creation is permissionless, this can be done deliberately: an attacker creates a second incentive on the same token, stakes their own position as sole staker, and unstakes at endTime with rewards[token][attacker] set to the full nominal figure — the difference comes out of the honest incentive's balance.
  • Rebasing tokens that contract, for the same reason, without anyone acting.
  • Blocklisting tokens (USDC-style), where a blocklisted recipient makes claimReward revert permanently for that address. Also tokens with admin balance controls, including KAP-20's adminTransfer on KUB Chain, which lets the token developer move the reward balance out from under this contract entirely.

The attack is confined to reward tokens that are themselves non-standard — a clean ERC-20's incentive cannot be drained this way, because a shortfall can only be created in the token's own denomination. That confinement is what holds this at Medium.

Vulnerable code
uint256 totalReward = incentive.totalReward + reward;require(totalReward <= type(uint128).max, 'JunoswapV3Staker::createIncentive: reward too large'); // the books are credited with what was asked for, not with what arrivedincentive.totalReward = totalReward;incentive.totalRewardUnclaimed += reward; TransferHelperExtended.safeTransferFrom(address(key.rewardToken), msg.sender, address(this), reward);
Impact

Rewards owed to stakers of one incentive are paid out of another incentive's funds, and the last claimants find claimReward reverting permanently — a freeze of already-earned rewards with no admin recovery path, because this contract deliberately has no admin. LP principal is unaffected: the NFT withdrawal path does not touch the reward token at all, which is the single most important reason this is not High.

A deliberate version requires the honest incentive to already be denominated in a fee-on-transfer token, in which case it was broken before the attacker arrived. The realistic exposure is an incentive creator picking a token without knowing its transfer semantics.

Recommendation

Credit what actually arrived rather than what was asked for. Measure the contract's balance before and after the transfer and use the difference — three extra lines, no behavioural change for a well-behaved token, and it makes the per-incentive books match the real balance by construction.

This does not fix blocklists or admin-controlled balances, which cannot be fixed from this side. For those, document the constraint clearly for incentive creators: the reward token must be a standard ERC-20 with no transfer fee, no rebasing, no blocklist and no privileged balance movement. On KUB Chain in particular, audit the KAP-20 committee address of any token used as a reward, per kub.md.

Also consider rejecting a reward token whose balanceOf does not increase at all, which catches the pathological cases early rather than at the first claim.

L-01 · Unchecked arithmetic in solc 0.7.6 — the uint128 reward cap can be wrapped past, and stakedLiquidity accumulates unchecked

Fixed

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:108-113 (createIncentive), :351 (_stakeToken), :228 (unstakeToken)

Description

The contract is pinned to solc 0.7.6, where arithmetic wraps silently rather than reverting, and it imports no SafeMath. Upstream is the same, but two of the additions the fork makes are affected.

The reward cap defeats itself. uint256 totalReward = incentive.totalReward + reward; is unchecked, so a reward close to type(uint256).max wraps the sum to a small value that passes require(totalReward <= type(uint128).max) — the very guard whose comment says it keeps the accumulator clear of overflow — while incentive.totalRewardUnclaimed += reward records the enormous figure. This requires a token whose supply approaches 2^256, so it is not reachable against any real token, and an attacker-authored token only affects its own incentive. But the check the fork added does not hold under its own arithmetic.

stakedLiquidity accumulates unchecked. incentive.stakedLiquidity += liquidity at line 351 is a uint128 sum over an unbounded number of positions. Uniswap caps a single position at maxLiquidityPerTick, so the sum wraps only after roughly numTicks maximum-liquidity positions — 8,874 at the 1% fee tier, 29,576 at 0.3%, 177,456 at 0.05%. Each such position costs dust to mint (see H-02) but a full stake transaction to add, so the gas is the real barrier: several billion gas at the 1% tier. Affordable on a cheap-gas chain, laborious anywhere. Once wrapped, the matching -= at line 228 underflows on unstake and the accumulator denominator becomes meaningless; rewards are lost, though the NFTs still come out and no function bricks.

Note that the two uint32(secondsInside - initial) casts are *not* part of this finding — that is intentional modular arithmetic on a Uniswap accumulator designed to wrap, and it is correct as written in 0.7.6.

Vulnerable code
uint256 totalReward = incentive.totalReward + reward;// 0.7.6 does not check this addition, so a reward near type(uint256).max wraps the sum to a// small value and passes the very guard meant to bound the accumulatorrequire(totalReward <= type(uint128).max, 'JunoswapV3Staker::createIncentive: reward too large'); ... incentive.stakedLiquidity += liquidity;  // unchecked uint128 sum over unbounded positions
Impact

Neither path is reachable against a standard reward token in a normally-sized incentive, and neither can freeze funds or trap an LP NFT. The reward-cap wrap harms only the incentive created with the pathological token. The stakedLiquidity wrap corrupts reward accounting for one incentive at a cost of billions of gas, with no gain to the attacker beyond the griefing already available far more cheaply through H-02.

Reported because the guard the fork introduced does not do what its comment claims, and because that comment is load-bearing for the accumulator-overflow argument in liveness check 6.

Recommendation

Use LowGasSafeMath — already a dependency via @uniswap/v3-core — for both additions, so a wrap reverts instead of passing the guard. For stakedLiquidity, add the sum in uint256 and downcast through an explicit bound check, since LowGasSafeMath does not cover uint128.

If the contract is ever ported to solc 0.8, the two uint32(secondsInside - stake.secondsInsideInitial) expressions must be wrapped in unchecked blocks. They are designed to wrap, and 0.8's checked arithmetic would turn them into a permanent revert on unstakeToken — converting a deliberate no-op into a bricked withdrawal path. Note this in the code before anyone attempts the upgrade.

I-01 · Informational — timestamp horizon, trapped ETH, misleading reverts, and the missing test surface

Open

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:103, :121-133, :250-264, :158-167; hardhat.config.js; test/

Description

Seven items with no security impact individually. None needs to block a deployment; all are cheap to close.

1. Incentives cannot end after 2106. require(key.endTime <= type(uint32).max) at line 103 caps endTime at 2106-02-07, so stakeTime and secondsInsideInitial fit in uint32. Correct and deliberate, but the horizon is not documented in the NatSpec and createIncentive will begin rejecting long-dated incentives well before that date. State the limit in the interface.

2. multicall is payable but nothing is. Multicall.multicall from v3-periphery is payable, while this contract has no payable function and no withdrawal path for ETH. Any ETH sent alongside a multicall is permanently trapped. Inherited from upstream; worth an explicit require(msg.value == 0) override, or a note for integrators.

3. endIncentive reverts confusingly when everything was distributed. require(refund > 0) is checked *before* require(numberOfStakes == 0), so an incentive that distributed its whole budget reverts with 'no refund available' and never emits IncentiveEnded. Harmless — there is nothing to refund — but indexers relying on the event will never see the incentive close. Consider emitting the event and returning zero instead of reverting.

4. claimReward(token, to, 0) claims everything. amountRequested == 0 is the 'claim all' sentinel. Inherited from upstream and documented nowhere in this interface; an integrator passing a computed zero amount will move the entire balance. Document it or use type(uint256).max as the sentinel instead.

5. onERC721Received's data.length == 160 heuristic is fragile. It distinguishes a single IncentiveKey from an array by encoded length. Correct today because IncentiveKey is five static fields, but it breaks silently if a field is ever added or made dynamic. Inherited from upstream. Add a comment tying the constant to the struct's field count.

6. The keys loop in onERC721Received has no explicit cap. Self-limiting — an over-long array only exhausts the caller's own gas — so not a DoS. Noted for completeness because it is the only loop in the contract.

7. There are no on-chain tests. test/reward-model.test.js is a JavaScript re-implementation of the accounting and it passes, but nothing exercises the compiled bytecode against a real Uniswap V3 pool. snapshotCumulativesInside — the external call every reward calculation depends on, and the one at the centre of H-01 — is never invoked in any test. hardhat.config.js declares no networks and there is no deployment script.

Impact

None individually. Item 7 is the one that matters in aggregate: the reward model is new code with no test coverage against the primitive it is built on, which is why H-01 was reachable — the project's own model tests the case where a position is in range throughout (case 7) but not the case where it is in range only after endTime.

Recommendation

Items 1, 4 and 5: document in the NatSpec of IJunoswapV3Staker. Item 2: override multicall with require(msg.value == 0). Item 3: emit IncentiveEnded with a zero refund rather than reverting.

Item 7 is the substantive one. Build three layers:

  • Fork tests against a real V3 pool — mint positions, move the price with real swaps, stake, let time pass, unstake, and assert the payouts. This is the layer that would have caught H-01 and H-02.
  • Foundry invariant tests on the two properties this contract must never violate: sum(all payouts) + totalRewardUnclaimed == totalReward for every incentive, and every deposited NFT is withdrawable — the latter matters more than usual here, because liveness check 7 notes there is no emergency exit behind the reward math.
  • Slither in CI, which this audit could not run.

Add forge test --gas-report or forge snapshot at the same time so the gas cost of any fix to H-01/H-02 is visible.

Roles, permissions and external calls

There is no privileged role in this contract. No owner, no Ownable, no roles, no initialize, no proxy, no pause, no sweep, no migrator, no upgrade path. The four immutables (factory, nonfungiblePositionManager, maxIncentiveStartLeadTime, maxIncentiveDuration) are set in the constructor and can never change.

That removes the entire centralization catalog from staking.md and methodology.md, and it is worth stating plainly: there is no key whose compromise costs anyone anything, and no admin who can rug. The closest thing to a privileged party is key.refundee, and it is not privileged at all — it is a field of the incentive key, fixed at creation and hashed into the incentive id. endIncentive may be called by anyone but always pays that address.

External-call inventory — all to the pinned Uniswap contracts or the incentive's own reward token: nonfungiblePositionManager.positions (view, ×2), nonfungiblePositionManager.safeTransferFrom (in withdrawToken, after state is cleared), pool.snapshotCumulativesInside (view, ×3), pool.slot0 (view), and rewardToken.transfer / transferFrom via TransferHelperExtended. No call is made inside a loop, none forwards a fixed gas stipend, and no require(success) sits on a call an untrusted party controls.

Reentrancy: there is no nonReentrant modifier anywhere, and none is needed. The two paths that hand control to an external party — withdrawToken's safeTransferFrom and claimReward's transfer — both write state before the call (delete deposits[tokenId], rewards[...] -= reward). Re-entering stakeToken from an ERC-721 receiver hook finds deposits[tokenId].owner == address(0) and reverts. A malicious reward token re-entering claimReward finds its balance already zeroed. onERC721Received is gated to the position manager.

Value flow — the money map

Every unit of reward token entering this contract leaves by one of exactly two doors, and the accounting between them conserves.

In. createIncentive pulls reward from the creator and credits both incentive.totalReward (the drip basis, effectively frozen once the incentive starts) and incentive.totalRewardUnclaimed (the live pot).

Accrual. _accrue grows rewardPerLiquidityX128 by totalReward × dt × 2^128 / (duration × stakedLiquidity) for each interval between stake/unstake events, and only while stakedLiquidity > 0. Seconds during which nothing is staked accrue nothing — by design, so a latecomer cannot snipe the idle period. This is correct, and the project's own model covers it (case 8).

Out, door 1 — stakers. unstakeToken converts a stake's accumulator delta into rewards[rewardToken][deposit.owner] and decrements totalRewardUnclaimed by the same amount; claimReward then transfers it out. Both FullMath.mulDiv calls round down, and computeRewardAmount clamps at totalRewardUnclaimed, so the sum of all payouts can never exceed totalReward. Conservation was verified algebraically (each staker's share is l / stakedLiquidity of the drip, and the shares sum to one) and by running the project's model.

Out, door 2 — the refundee. endIncentive sends whatever is left of totalRewardUnclaimed to key.refundee. Three things end up here: reward for seconds when nothing was staked, reward forfeited by out-of-range uptime, and rounding dust.

Where the findings bite. Door 2 is far wider than the design intends: H-02 shows it takes the whole budget in the adversarial case and a proportional slice of it in ordinary operation, while H-01 is the opposite leak — a staker taking from door 2 what it did not earn. Neither moves LP principal; the LP NFT is separate custody with its own exit.

Loop and gas analysis

There is exactly one loop in the codebase.

JunoswapV3Staker.sol:163 — for (uint256 i = 0; i < keys.length; i++) in onERC721Received, iterating the incentive keys a depositor asked to stake into in one transfer. The bound is controlled by *the caller, for their own token only*: an over-long array makes the caller's own safeTransferFrom run out of gas and revert, harming nobody else. No other user's funds, and no function anyone else needs, depends on this loop terminating. Verdict: safe, not a finding. It is upstream code, unchanged.

There is no loop over stakers, positions, pools or reward tokens anywhere. The rewardPerLiquidityX128 accumulator is exactly the O(1)-per-user structure gas.md asks for, and payouts are pull-based via rewards[token][owner] + claimReward. Both structural liveness bugs that catalog warns about — push payments in a loop, unbounded iteration in a required function — are absent by construction.

Gas as a griefing surface appears not in a loop but in the eviction asymmetry behind H-02 and H-03: an attacker can stake k dust positions in one multicall, while clearing them costs k separate unstakeToken transactions. That is the only place in this contract where one party can make another party's necessary work grow, and on a cheap-gas chain it is affordable to sustain indefinitely.

Deployment size is not a concern — 364 lines with no heavy inlined libraries, far under the EIP-170 limit.

No gas *optimizations* are reported. The contract is already tight; the one place it spends more than upstream (deviation 6, ~20k extra per stake for the unpacked Stake struct) buys correctness, and gas.md is explicit that a bug introduced by a gas tweak is not a saving.

Liveness — the seven positive checks

arithmetic.md requires every audited contract to answer these explicitly. This one answers yes to six of seven, and the seventh is a deliberate trade-off rather than a defect.

The headline: an LP can always get their NFT back. unstakeToken → withdrawToken touches only the pool (two views) and the position manager (one safeTransferFrom). It reads no oracle, needs no admin, cannot be paused, and does not touch the reward token at all — so even a completely broken reward token cannot trap an NFT. We tried to construct a state in which that path reverts and could not.

Quantified — reward per dollar by range width (M-01)

Method: closed form. For a position of liquidity L around price P spanning a relative price width W, the deposited value in token1 terms is approximately L × √P × W / 2, so L = 2V / (√P × W). Reward weight in this contract is L alone, so reward per unit of capital scales as 1 / W. The reference is a ±10% range (W = 0.2222); the comparison is the tightest range each fee tier permits, W = 1.0001^tickSpacing − 1.

The last column answers the practical question: how much capital, posted at the minimum width, earns the same reward as $100,000 posted at ±10%.

This behaviour is inherited from upstream — Uniswap's own staker also weights by raw liquidity — so it is not a fork-introduced defect, and it is filed at Medium for that reason. It is reported because the contract's stated goal is that a launchpad's fixed reward reaches its participants, and at the 0.01% tier a $45 position collects what a $100,000 position collects.

Remediation roadmap

Tier 0 (gate)The reward model's in-range accountinghard gate

Subtract post-endTime wall time from the credited in-range seconds (H-01 — one expression, no new state). Reject out-of-range positions at stake time and bound a stake's credited liquidity against the pool's own active liquidity (H-02). While either is open, an incentive funded through this contract can be stripped of its refund or denied its distribution for the price of gas.

Closes: H-01 H-02

→ The budget reaches the stakers it was meant for, and dust cannot suppress it

Tier 1The permissionless eviction path

Replace the spot slot0.tick test in _isOutOfRange with a condition a single block cannot manufacture — a TWAP tick from pool.observe(), or a minimum out-of-range dwell measured from two secondsInside snapshots. Consider removing the path outright once H-02 is fixed at source, since working around H-02 is the only reason it exists.

Closes: H-03

→ No caller can clear competing stakers out of an incentive with a swap

Tier 2Token and arithmetic robustness

Measure the reward-token balance before and after in createIncentive and credit what actually arrived (M-02). Route every + and - through LowGasSafeMath, starting with incentive.totalReward + reward and incentive.stakedLiquidity += liquidity (L-01).

Closes: M-02 L-01

→ Per-incentive accounting cannot drift from the contract's real balance

Tier 3Distribution fairness and the untested surface

Decide explicitly whether a minimum range width or a width-weighted reward is wanted (M-01) — a product decision, defensible either way, but it should be a stated one. Then address I-01 and build the test suite the project does not have: fork tests against a real V3 pool, plus Foundry invariant tests for sum(payouts) + totalRewardUnclaimed == totalReward and every deposited NFT is withdrawable. Run Slither, which this audit could not.

Closes: M-01 I-01

→ Distribution matches the stated intent, and regressions surface in CI rather than on-chain
